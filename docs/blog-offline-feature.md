# Now the karaoke converter works with no internet at all

When I brought the CDG to MP4 converter back, the whole point was that it runs in your browser instead of on a server. No uploads, nothing to break. This week I pushed that idea one step further: it now works completely offline, and you can install it like a real app.

Karaoke does not happen in places with good wifi. It happens in basements, backyards, dive bars, community halls, and the back seat of a car on the way to the party. The old web version needed a connection just to load, which always felt wrong for a tool people use in exactly those spots.

So now you can open the converter once, and from then on it just works, internet or not.

## Install it like an app

On your phone or laptop, your browser will offer to install Karaokio to your home screen or dock. Tap that, and you get an icon that opens straight into the converter, full screen, no address bar. It behaves like any other app you have, except there is no app store and nothing to update by hand.

## It runs with the wifi off

Once it has loaded, the converter and everything it needs live on your device. Turn off the wifi, go to airplane mode, walk into a basement with zero bars. Drop in a karaoke file and it still converts. Your files never leave your device, because there is nowhere for them to go.

## You can see when it is ready, and you are in control

There is a little status pill at the bottom. When the converter is saved for offline use, it shows a green dot that says "Available offline." That is your "this will work on the plane" signal.

If you want the space back, there is a Clear button right next to it. One tap removes the saved converter, about thirty megabytes, and the dot goes grey. Want it back later, you just tap to save it again, or it saves itself the next time you convert something. No settings to dig through. You decide what lives on your device.

## A couple of honest notes

The first time you save it for offline, your browser downloads the converter engine once. It is around thirty megabytes, because it is a real, full copy of the tool that runs the conversion. After that first time it is cached, so it loads instantly and works with no connection. If your device ever runs low on space, the browser may quietly clear it to make room, and the converter will simply download it again next time you are online. Nothing breaks, nothing gets stuck.

When I ship an update, the app notices on its own and offers a "tap to reload" so you are never stuck on an old version, and it will never interrupt a conversion to do it.

That is the whole feature. Install it, use it anywhere, clear it whenever you want. The simplest tools are the ones that keep working when everything else has no signal.

Go sing somewhere with bad wifi. That is kind of the point.
