## X / Twitter thread

1/ For seven years the only analytics on my karaoke CDG-to-MP4 converter was people emailing me when it broke. This summer I rebuilt it (Claude did most of the typing) and set up PostHog for the first time. One month of data in.

2/ The numbers: 111 people from 15+ countries, 490 successful conversions, and 93% of them got downloaded. People who convert a song want that song.

3/ What I didn't expect: five people did 86% of the recent volume. One person in Florida converted 181 songs in a weekend. Queen, Journey, Prince, Meat Loaf. A whole classic-rock collection.

4/ The filenames still carry old karaoke disc catalog codes (Sound Choice, DK, EK). These are people with binders of CD+G discs, digitizing them for modern playback. Nothing modern plays those discs.

5/ Every one of them has duplicate conversions in their history. No batch mode means feeding songs in one at a time and losing track. Someone sat through 181 individual conversions. So batch mode is the next feature. I didn't need a survey to find it.

6/ If you have a small tool in the world and no idea who uses it: instrument it. At this scale the data reads like a story about five real people. Full writeup: [LINK]

## LinkedIn

I rebuilt my old karaoke file converter this summer and added product analytics (PostHog) for the first time. The tool had run for seven years with no telemetry beyond support emails.

One month of data later:

111 users from 15+ countries. 490 conversions, 93% of which ended in a download. One finding changed my roadmap: five users generated 86% of the volume, each digitizing a commercial karaoke disc collection one song at a time, with duplicate conversions showing they lost track along the way. One user converted 181 songs in a weekend.

I did not need a survey or an interview to pick the next feature. Batch mode was sitting in the event stream.

If you maintain a small tool and have no idea who uses it, instrument it. At small scale the data is legible in a way dashboards at big companies never are.
